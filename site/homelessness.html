Homelessness: dollars and reported outcomes

This page shows what is spent and what the state reports, side by side. It cannot show that the spending caused any change: counts also move with housing costs, the economy and how and when people are counted. The State Auditor found in 2024 that California has not consistently tracked either the cost or the results of its homelessness programs, and that San Diego could not total all of its own homelessness spending. Every dollar figure here is therefore a floor, and every outcome is "reported", not "effective".

What the city spends

The City's Homelessness Strategies & Solutions Department, from each year's adopted budget (actual spending for 2021 to 2024, then the budget and adopted amounts). "Per person counted" divides by the people counted on one night in January of that year. That count covers the whole county, not just the city, so these per-person figures understate the city's spending per person counted in the city. The department's revenue (in the budget, charges for services and transfers such as hotel-tax money, not grants) is shown beside its spending, not netted off. The budget does not say which funds, such as the state HHAP grants below, paid for which spending, so city and state dollars are shown separately and never added together. Other city departments, such as police and libraries, also spend on homelessness and are not included.

The department changed shape over these years (2021 includes one-off pandemic funds), so compare years with care.

What the state awarded

Awards from the state's Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention (HHAP) program, rounds 1 to 5: the amounts set aside for the regional homelessness body, the city and the county, added together for each area. These are allocations, not money spent, and HHAP is only one state program. Each round's amount is divided by the count its allocation was based on (the year in the table). Because the state split each round in proportion to counts, the per-person amounts come out almost the same in every area by design. Areas the state approved to use an older (2017) count, which includes San Diego for rounds 1 to 3, are divided by that count. The remaining differences are mostly how the state split large counties: Los Angeles County's award reflects the whole county, including areas with their own regional bodies, but is divided here by the Los Angeles count only, so Los Angeles can look lower per person than it is.

How many people are counted

The one-night count each January, shown relative to 2019 (2019 = 100) because the areas differ in size. A "region" is the area the federal count covers, usually a city plus its county, so it is larger than the city. 2021 is blank for most regions, San Diego included: the federal government waived the unsheltered count that year, and only some regions counted. A hollow dot marks a year in which a region counted only its shelters and HUD reused its last unsheltered count. Dashed lines are reference areas (Arlington County VA and Vancouver WA), included because they matter to the reader, not as peers.

Sources and limits

Counts and outcomes come from the California Interagency Council on Homelessness (system performance measures built from each region's records) and from HUD's point-in-time counts. HHAP awards are from the Department of Housing and Community Development's allocation tables. City amounts are from the City's adopted budgets. Comparing regions is not comparing city governments: the state's regions cover different areas and spend through counties, cities and nonprofits. See Data & methods.
